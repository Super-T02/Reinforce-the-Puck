\subsection{New Replay Buffers} \label{sec:buffers}

The basic approach for modeling experience buffers are \ac{ER} buffers \cite{lin1992ER}, which hold the past $n$ (most of the time $n>100000$) transitions. At training time, $m$ transition batches are sampled to train actor and critic networks. Although each iteration is sampled uniformly, early observations in the empty buffer  are drawn more often than late experiences \cite{schaul2015prioritized}. Instead, we propose first \ac{BER} and  \ac{PER} \cite{schaul2015prioritized}, to ensure a balanced or prioritized sampling of late and new experiences.

\paragraph{\acl{BER}} \ac{BER} is a balanced version of the default \ac{ER} where each experience gets a corresponding memory strength. This strength is reduced every time a new experience is added to the  buffer. The resulting probability to draw an experience $j$ from the buffer is $P(j) = \frac{s_j^\alpha}{\sum_{i}s_i^\alpha}$, where $s_i$ corresponds to the strength of experience $i$.

\paragraph{\acl{PER}} \ac{PER} is introduced by \cite{schaul2015prioritized} and focuses on prioritizing experiences with a big \ac{TD}-error. Thus, each experience $j$ has a sampling probability $P(j) = \frac{\delta_j^\alpha}{\sum_{i}\delta_i^\alpha}$, where $\delta_i$ is the \ac{TD}-error of experience $i$ and $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter describing the influence of the priorities. In general, each metric can be used to assign priorities, which may lead to a bias concerning the sampled data. To further balance the sampling, we implement the buffer as Sum Trees, which allow sampling and adding data in $O(log\,n)$  \cite{schaul2015prioritized}.

% \paragraph{\acl{BPER}} \ac{BPER} \cite{Sun2022:BPER} is a combination of balancing the probability by the memory strength and assigning a priority to the experience. Therefore, we use effective priorities $e_i = p^\alpha_i \cdot s_i$, where $p_i$ is the priority and $s_i$ the memory strength of sample $i$. These effective priorities are then translated into the sampling probability. Furthermore, we use the normalized reward $r_i' = \frac{r_i - \min_j r_j}{\max_j r_j - \min_j r_j}$ as $p_i$, because we want to sample experiences with the highest rewards. After sampling the experiences, the old rewards of the sampled data are updated with the new rewards.
